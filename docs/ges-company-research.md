# GES (Global Experience Specialists): Company Research

> **Purpose:** Background research on GES ([ges.com](https://www.ges.com)) to support building a mergers & acquisitions (M&A) application for its buy-and-build strategy.
> **Research date:** November 19, 2026
> **Sources:** Public web sources only (company site, press releases, trade press, SEC filings). See [Sources](#sources). Figures are as reported publicly and have not been independently audited.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **What GES does:** A global live-events company providing exhibition and trade show services ("general services contractor" work), experiential marketing / custom exhibits, event housing, registration and data, and, as of November 2026, association marketing.
- **Who owns it:** **Truelink Capital**, a Los Angeles middle-market private equity firm. Truelink bought GES from **Viad Corp** (now Pursuit, NYSE: PRSU) for **$535M in cash**. The deal was announced October 21, 2024 and closed around December 31, 2024.
- **Scale:** About **$888M revenue** and **$68M Adjusted EBITDA** in FY2023, the last full year reported publicly. Viad guided FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA to **$85–95M**, which puts the purchase multiple at about **5.9x EBITDA**. Other figures: **~2,600 employees**, **4,000 live events a year**, **150K exhibitors served a year**, work in **75+ countries**.
- **M&A posture: actively acquiring.** Truelink's thesis from day one was growth "organically and through strategic acquisitions." Two add-ons have closed so far:
  1. **2Heads** (London experiential agency), December 2025, folded into Spiro.
  2. **CSG Creative** (Alexandria, VA association marketing agency), November 5, 2026, run as a standalone operating company.
- **Signals of more deals:** At the CSG close, Truelink said it continues "to see a **robust pipeline of M&A opportunities** to build on the platform." In mid-2025 Truelink's Luke Myers said: *"From a consolidation standpoint, we are in the **3rd inning**… the opportunity is even larger than we expected."*
- **Where the deals point:** (a) building out the agency/creative layer, (b) moving "up the event lifecycle" toward organizers (attendee marketing, data, tech), and (c) expanding internationally. Tech-enabled and data capabilities are an explicit priority.

**Notes:**
- FY2021 shows the COVID trough; FY2022 shows the rebound.
- The FY2023 revenue increase absorbed a **~$74M headwind** from the 2022 sale of a non-core audio-visual business (ON Services) and from the timing of non-annual shows.
- **Deal math:** $535M ÷ ~$90M midpoint EBITDA ≈ **5.9x**. That is a low multiple for a market leader and leaves room for multiple arbitrage on bolt-ons and an eventual exit.
- **Deal structure:** $510M cash at close, plus $25M deferred for one year (contingent only on time passing). Price subject to cash, debt and working-capital adjustments.
- **Business model traits:** Revenue is lumpy and seasonal, and depends on show rotation (non-annual mega-shows such as IMTS and MINExpo). Margins are thin in exhibitions and better in agency work. Labor is heavily unionized.

## 6. History & Acquisition Track Record

GES was itself built through acquisitions. This history shows how it integrates targets: it either **merges them into the GES brand** or **keeps them as branded operating companies**.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1939 | Manncraft (signs, windows, small exhibits) founded in Kansas City |
| 1950s | Exhibitor Service Division created; expands to Omaha, Miami, Tampa, Atlanta, New Orleans |
| 1969 | Greyhound Corp. buys Manncraft and renames it **Greyhound Exhibition Services (GES)** |
| 1992 | HQ moves to Las Vegas; 500+ full-time staff |
| 1993 | **Acquires United Exposition Service Co.** to go nationwide; renamed GES Exposition Services |
| 1995 | **Enters Canada** via Panex Show Services and Stampede Display & Convention Services |
| 1996 | Dial Corp splits; **Viad Corp** formed to hold service businesses including GES |
| 2007 | **Acquires Melville Exhibition & Event Services (UK)** |
| 2008 | Melville expands to ADNEC (Abu Dhabi); **acquires Becker Group** (experiential marketing) |
| 2010 | Merges Exhibitgroup/Giltspur, Becker and GES; renamed **Global Experience Specialists** |
| 2014 | **Acquires Blitz Communications** (UK AV), **onPeak and Travel Planners** (housing), **Visit** (EU registration and data) |
| 2018 | Name shortened to "GES" |
| 2022 | **Spiro** brand created; non-core AV business (ON Services) divested |
| Oct 2024 | Truelink signs a $535M deal to buy GES from Viad |
| Dec 2024 | Deal closes; Viad becomes **Pursuit** (NYSE: PRSU) |
| **Dec 2025** | **Acquires 2Heads** (London; offices in LA and Montreal). First add-on under Truelink; joins Spiro |
| **Nov 2026** | **Acquires CSG Creative** from National Trade Productions. Second add-on; standalone brand |

### The two Truelink-era deals in detail

**2Heads (December 2, 2025)**
- London-based experiential and creative agency with 40+ years of history and offices in LA and Montreal.
- Clients span technology, financial services, consumer and aerospace.
- **Rationale:** expand Spiro's creative footprint in the UK and Europe and deepen its strategic and digital capabilities.
- **Integration model:** absorbed into Spiro, with continuity for clients and teams. Managing Director Paul Godwin stays on.

**CSG Creative (November 5, 2026)**
- Headquartered in Alexandria, VA; nearly 25 years old; founded by Paige Cardwell.
- Revenue is mostly **year-round retainer** relationships.
- **Clients:** WEFTEC (Water Environment Federation), SHOT Show (NSSF), NTCA, AARP, American Chemical Society, ISA Sign Expo, SIOR.
- Generated 70M+ paid-media impressions and 580K+ clicks (July 2025 to June 2026).
- **Rationale:** give associations and organizers "a single partner from attendee marketing through show execution." The deal opens **two-way cross-selling**: CSG services to GES clients, and GES exhibition services to CSG clients.
- **Integration model:** standalone operating company; brand, team and leadership kept.
- Financial terms were not disclosed.

**Pattern:** Both targets are **founder- or legacy-led creative agencies** where people and client relationships are the main assets. The deals are relationship-sourced: Linde said "we've known Paige… for years." Each target fills a capability gap rather than adding scale in the core contracting business.

## 7. Strategy & M&A Thesis

From Truelink's and GES's public statements:

1. **Expand the agency vertical through Spiro.** "Strategic opportunity to further expand into the agency vertical through Spiro" was the headline reason for the original investment.
2. **Own more of the event lifecycle.** Move from show execution (floor, freight, booths) upstream into attendee acquisition, marketing, data and registration, and downstream into insights for brands.
3. **Tech-enabled offerings and data.** *"We're currently very focused on turning each event in a scalable data collection environment, with data from one event informing a better experience across our entire event portfolio."* (Luke Myers). Truelink wants to "enhance GES' tech-enabled offerings to improve the customer experience."
4. **International expansion.** "Eager to explore global opportunities to further expand GES' international reach." 2Heads added UK/EU agency depth; GES already operates in the Middle East (ADNEC, Dubai) and India.
5. **Consolidate a fragmented industry.** Myers says the industry is in the "3rd inning" of consolidation. He also cites **high barriers to entry** and "durable" demand with "favorable tailwinds."
6. **Diligence red flag Truelink cares about:** *"The biggest red flag is when a target misses performance while in market."*

### Likely target categories (inferred from the above)

| Category | Why it fits | Example profile |
|---|---|---|
| Experiential / brand activation agencies | Adds scale and geography to Spiro | Regional U.S. or EU agencies, $5–50M revenue |
| Association and organizer marketing, content, sponsorship sales | Builds on CSG; cross-sell into GES organizer base | Attendee-acquisition shops, sponsorship brokers |
| Event tech: registration, lead retrieval, matchmaking, analytics, AI | Data thesis; complements Visit | SaaS or tech-enabled services with event data |
| Regional general services contractors | Market-share roll-up in core exhibitions | Independent GSCs in secondary U.S. markets |
| Specialty contractors: electrical, AV, rigging, signage and graphics, furniture rental | Margin capture on exhibitor orders | Local specialty vendors |
| Housing and travel management | Builds on onPeak | Housing bureaus |
| International exhibition services | Geographic expansion (EU, Middle East, India, APAC) | In-country contractors |

## 8. Competitive Landscape

GES is one of two national "big general services contractors" in North America. The rest of the market is fragmented and increasingly PE-backed.

| Competitor | Ownership | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Freeman** | Private (family-owned), Dallas | Market leader and GES's main rival. Long history of roll-ups (e.g., bought Champion Exposition Services, George Fern and Immersa in 2011). Owns Sparks (experiential) |
| **Fern Exposition & Event Services** | PE, **MSouth Equity Partners** (2023) | Cincinnati; services 1,400+ shows a year; paired with Nth Degree under MSouth |
| **Nth Degree** | PE, MSouth | Trade show labor, exhibit management and events; 4,000+ events a year |
| **Shepard Exposition Services** | Employee-owned, Atlanta | 120+ years old; serial acquirer (Markey's Exposition Services; reportedly US Expo in 2026) |
| **Heritage Exposition Services** | Employee-owned | National GSC |
| **Alliance Nationwide Exposition** | Private, Kissimmee, FL | 201–500 employees; markets "fair, transparent" pricing |
| **The Expo Group** | Private | GSC plus experiential |
| **Smaller / regional GSCs** | Mostly founder-owned | e.g., American Exposition Services (Sacramento), Expo Convention Contractors (Miami), LUXX Exposition Services (Chicago), Hawaii Expo, Stronco (Canada) |

**Implications:**
- Regional, founder-owned GSCs and specialty contractors are the **natural roll-up pool**. Many of them show up at ESCA (Exhibition Services & Contractors Association) events.
- **Competition for deals is rising.** Other PE-backed platforms (MSouth's Fern and Nth Degree) and strategic buyers (Freeman, Shepard) are chasing the same targets.
- Industry associations GES belongs to are useful for deal sourcing: **ESCA, IAEE, SISO, PCMA, CEIR, AEO (UK), ESSA (UK), Women in Exhibitions**.

## 9. Industry Context (2025–2026)

- **The exhibitions market is in an expansion phase.** Organizers are launching and buying shows across the Middle East, Europe and the Americas, for example Easyfairs' first U.S. buy (EPC Expo), Fairfest buying WTE Miami, and Terrapinn launching five shows in Saudi Arabia.
- **Private equity activity is high** across organizers and suppliers. Global M&A value rose about 49% year-over-year in 2025, the strongest year since 2021.
- **Structural tailwinds:** in-person events have a durable role ("AI gets you seen, live events build trust"), and brands are shifting marketing budgets toward experiential.
- **Risks** (from Viad's disclosures): seasonality and show-rotation timing; union labor cost and work stoppages; transportation costs; key-person dependency on account teams; integration risk on acquisitions; cybersecurity and data-privacy (relevant to the registration and data business); FX exposure; macro and travel disruption.

## 10. Implications for an M&A Application

These are the needs this research suggests an M&A tool for GES should address.

### Who would use it
- **GES corporate development and executive team:** Derek Linde and the segment presidents.
- **Truelink deal team:** Luke Myers and Peter Lewis (Head of Business Development).
- **Integration leads** in Spiro, Exhibitions, and the standalone operating companies.

### Core features to consider

1. **Target pipeline / CRM**
   - Stages: Sourced → Contacted → NDA → IOI → LOI → Diligence → Signed → Closed → Integrating.
   - Tag each target by **category** (agency, GSC, specialty contractor, event tech, housing, marketing, international) and by **which GES brand it would join** (Spiro, Exhibitions, onPeak, Visit, standalone).
   - Track **relationship history**, since deals are relationship-sourced: who knows the founder, and the last touchpoint.
2. **Target profiles**
   - Revenue, EBITDA, margin, headcount, locations, founder/owner, ownership type (founder, family, ESOP, PE).
   - Key clients and shows, with **client overlap against GES's book** (for cross-sell and concentration risk).
   - Union exposure.
3. **Valuation and deal modeling**
   - EV/EBITDA comps, with GES's own ~5.9x entry multiple as a reference point.
   - Accretion and multiple-arbitrage math, earn-outs and deferred consideration (GES's own purchase used a deferred $25M tranche).
   - Synergy estimates: cross-sell revenue and cost savings.
4. **Diligence tracker**
   - Checklists, a document request list and red-flag logging.
   - In particular, **"performance vs. forecast while in market,"** which is Truelink's #1 stated deal-killer.
5. **Integration playbooks**
   - Two templates matching GES's patterns:
     - **Fold-in** (e.g., 2Heads into Spiro).
     - **Standalone operating company** (e.g., CSG).
   - Track retention of key people, client continuity and cross-sell wins.
6. **Market map and competitive intelligence**
   - Who owns what: Freeman, Fern/Nth Degree (MSouth), Shepard, etc.
   - Flag targets being pursued by competing platforms.
   - Feeds from TSNN, Exhibit City News and PRNewswire.
7. **Portfolio dashboard**
   - Closed deals, capital deployed, realized vs. planned synergies, and progress toward the platform's eventual exit.

### Seed data already available
- **Closed deals:** 2Heads (Dec 2025) and CSG Creative (Nov 2026), plus the historical acquisitions in Section 6.
- **Competitor and market-map entities:** Section 8.
- **GES's own baseline:** Section 4 financials.
